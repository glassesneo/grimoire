# Decision Rebase

## Purpose

Decisions made in a conversation rest on something: a goal, a constraint, a fact, another decision. When that base moves, the decisions that rested on it are in reach of the change, and each has to be placed again on the new base. Some hold as they are, some have lost their reason, and for some only the user knows.

The failure this skill corrects is placing them silently. The common form is carrying every earlier decision forward as fixed, so that the work bends around constraints nobody chose for the present situation. Dropping a decision without saying so is the same failure in the other direction. Either way the agent has decided on the user's behalf what a change meant.

A decision in reach is therefore open until it is settled with the user. A decision out of reach stays settled.

## When the Base Moves

The base moves in five ways. Each sets how far the reach extends and how to begin.

- The user asks to rethink the approach. Everything decided so far is in reach, back to the earliest decision and including those never discussed.
- The user changes a decision. In reach is whatever depended on it.
- The user says something that conflicts with an earlier decision without marking it as a change. They may have changed their mind, may not have the earlier decision in view, or may see no conflict where you see one. Name the two statements and ask which holds before treating it as a change; saying what each answer would put in reach helps the user answer.
- A finding, yours or the user's, removes what a decision rested on: an interface that does not exist, a measurement, a requirement that reads differently from how it was taken. In reach is whatever rested on it.
- Keeping a decision has come to cost more than was known when it was made: workarounds accumulate to honor it, or it excludes something the user now asks for. Nothing has moved except what is known about its price. State the decision and the cost observed, in a few sentences, and leave reopening it to the user. If the user keeps the decision, it stands at that price; raise it again only when the price grows.

When nothing decided earlier is in reach, continue the work. Most turns refine or extend what stands and move no base.

## Decisions and Facts

A decision in reach can be either of these:

- what the user stated, chose, or agreed to
- what you adopted without discussion and have treated as given since: compatibility preserved, an existing structure followed, a convention kept, a scope assumed. The user never saw these being decided, so name them when they are in reach.

A fact is not a decision and does not open: an external contract, a platform limit, data that already exists. The response to a fact is a decision: whether to preserve, migrate, or break compatibility can be in reach while the contract itself stays as it is. Check a fact again when the change could have touched it.

Your own choices with no consequence the user would see follow whatever is settled; revise them without asking.

## Placing Each Decision

Place each decision in reach by the reason it was made, as the conversation states it.

- The reason does not involve what moved: the decision stands.
- The reason was what moved: the decision has lost its base. The user may still want it for another reason; that is theirs to say.
- The new base forces an answer: say what is forced and by which verified fact.
- The reason is only partly involved, or the conversation does not state it: the decision is the user's to place.

A decision that is released also releases what it had excluded. Before adjusting the current design to fit, look at the directions that were ruled out because of it; they are available again, and the adjusted design is only one of them.

## What to Return

Reply in the form the work under way already uses, at a size that matches the reach. One decision in reach takes a sentence or two inside the ordinary reply; a request to rethink everything takes the whole reply.

- What moved, as you read it.
- The decisions that stand, with the reason the change does not reach them. One line covers the obvious ones. Showing them lets the user correct a wrong placement.
- The decisions that lost their base or that only the user can place, as questions. Say what you would expect and why; do not act on it yet.
- The directions the released decisions open, each with what it would cost the decisions that stand. Choosing among them is the user's. When reasoning from what stands favors one, say so apart from the list.

Then continue on the settled base. Work that does not depend on an open decision need not wait for the answer. Work that does depend on one waits; when the user cannot answer before the work must proceed, take the path that is cheapest to undo and open your next reply with the decisions left open.

When every decision in reach stands and nothing opens, say that in a line and continue.
